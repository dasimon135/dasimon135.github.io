Undocumented Devicesand how to talk to them anyway

I write open-source Home Assistant integrations for hardware that shipped without one — devices that work perfectly well, but that the manufacturer only ever exposed through a phone app.

The pattern is always the same. Something on your wall speaks a private protocol to a vendor cloud, and nobody wrote down what it says. So you capture the traffic, work out what the bytes mean, and hand the result back to Home Assistant as ordinary entities.

Everything here is free, MIT-licensed, and runs entirely on your own network. No accounts, no vendor cloud, nothing leaving the house.

2.4 GHz Bluetooth Low Energy

daikin_madoka

Integration★ 11

A Daikin BRC1H “Madoka” wall thermostat, over Bluetooth. Temperature, mode, fan speed and filter alerts become ordinary Home Assistant entities — no Daikin account, no phone app.

The panel silently ignores any Bluetooth link it hasn’t paired with. No error, no refusal, just nothing. A good half of this project is that one sentence, handled properly.

The integration was originally mduran80’s work. This fork has carried it since — rewritten connection layer, the pairing handling above, and the ESPHome route.

ha-bluetooth-mesh

Integration★ 3

Bluetooth SIG Mesh lighting — Häfele Connect Mesh and other ThingOS gear — driven through the ESPHome Bluetooth proxies you already have. A pure-Python mesh stack, provisioning and all.

The vendor’s own gateway was discontinued. The lights were fine; only the way in had disappeared.

ha-bluesight

Diagnostics★ 1

Read-only, and it changes nothing. It shows which Bluetooth proxy is holding which connection slot, then names what it sees: deadlocks, ghost slots, pairing storms. Written after spending too long guessing.

433.92 MHz ISM band · one-way radio

ha-rf-fan

Integration★ 8

Ceiling fans that only ever came with a handset. You press each button once while it listens, and the fan turns into a Home Assistant entity — speeds, light, timers, direction, whatever your remote actually has.

It never decodes the protocol. It captures what your remote sends and sends it back, which is why it works with fans I have never seen.

ha-dooya

Integration★ 6

Dooya roller blinds and shutters, through an ESPHome CC1101 gateway or a Broadlink RM4 Pro. Position too — the motor reports nothing back, so it is timed from travel, and recalibrated every time a blind reaches an end stop.

How this works, if you’re about to open an issue

Support happens on GitHub, in the repository the problem belongs to. That is deliberate: an issue stays readable, searchable and linkable long after the conversation ends, which a forum thread does not. Say what hardware you have and bring the logs, and you’ll usually get an answer within the week.

Bug reports from people with hardware I don’t own are worth more than they look. Several of these projects support devices I have never touched, because someone was patient enough to run a test build and tell me what happened.

All of it is free, and it stays free. If something here saved you an afternoon, you can buy me a coffee — it pays for the hardware I test on. Nothing else changes.
